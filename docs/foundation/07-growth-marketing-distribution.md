##### **Growth thesis** 

The shortest path to category leadership is not “market harder.” It is to make every verified opportunity a reusable distribution object: a searchable page, a shareable preview, a social post, a notification candidate and a personalized recommendation. 

### **2. Growth North Star and Lifecycle** 

SINNARA is a high-intent product. People do not need entertainment from it. They need useful opportunities at the right time. Growth should therefore optimize for qualified opportunity discovery and downstream action, not raw traffic. 

|**Stage**|**User state**|**SINNARA job**|**Primary metric**|
|---|---|---|---|

### **4. Acquisition Architecture** 

The MVP should use an owned, compound distribution system. Paid acquisition is deliberately excluded from the core launch engine until organic conversion and retention prove that SINNARA converts qualified demand efficiently.

#### **4.1 The acquisition principle** 

##### **Do not market SINNARA first. Market the value it contains.** 

A post saying “Try SINNARA” is weak. A post saying “5 programme roles closing this week, with direct official links” creates a reason to click. The product then converts that intent into personalized value.

### **7. Product-Led Acquisition and Signup Architecture** 

External opportunity page ↓ Read enough to trust SINNARA ↓ “See how this fits your profile” ↓ Sign up ↓ Upload CV ↓ Extraction + review ↓ See relevant opportunities ↓ Save / apply / alerts 

|**Funnel step**|**UX requirement**|**Primary risk**|**Optimization lever**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Landing|State concrete value in one<br>sentence|Generic job-board positioning|Opportunity-led headline + proof.|
|Signup|Minimum fields compatible with<br>auth|Abandonment before value|Social/OAuth or magic-link<br>options if reliable.|
|Upload|CV first, certificates optional|Users delay because profile forms<br>feel long|Single drop zone, accepted<br>formats visible.|
|Processing|Visible progress, page-safe<br>background state|Perceived slowness|Asynchronous processing +<br>return-safe state.|
|Review|Accept/edit claims quickly|AI extraction feels like homework|Bulk accept clear claims, targeted<br>corrections.|
|First value|Show relevant opportunities<br>immediately|Irrelevant first result|Quality gates before feed<br>publication.|

#### **7.1 Activation definition** 

A candidate is activated when SINNARA has enough validated profile data to generate at least one opportunity that passes hard eligibility and quality thresholds, and the user has been shown that recommendation. This is stronger than “account created” and more useful than “profile complete.”

#### **8.1 Referral design rules** 

- Do not require a referral to unlock core matching value. 

- Do not reveal private match explanations in a public referral URL. 

- Use attribution that survives one or two navigation steps without relying on cookies alone. 

- Reward referrals with utility, not artificial points. Example: invite a colleague to view the same opportunity or subscribe to a shared category digest. 

- Protect communities from spam. A partner should be able to opt out or set a preferred posting cadence. 

### **9. Retention & Re-engagement Architecture** 

Retention comes from changing opportunities and changing candidate context. SINNARA should avoid fake streaks, daily check-in mechanics and generic motivational notifications. 

|**Trigger**|**Eligibility**|**Message**|**Goal**|
|---|---|---|---|
|New strong match|New published job + candidate<br>threshold|“3 new opportunities matched<br>your profile.”|Return to review|
|Deadline approaching|Saved or highly matched<br>opportunity closing soon|“This saved opportunity closes<br>tomorrow.”|Drive action|
|Profile improved|User changed a material fact|“Your recommendations have<br>been updated.”|Demonstrate product<br>responsiveness|
|No strong matches|No high-quality current matches|“Nothing strong enough yet. Here<br>are 2 ways to widen your search.”|Help rather than spam|
|Application inactivity|Tracked application has no recent<br>user action|“Continue tracking your<br>application.”|Support organization|
|Career signal|Repeated demand for a missing<br>skill|“Power BI appears repeatedly in<br>roles you are exploring.”|Career intelligence|
|Weekly digest|User opted into weekly cadence|Top relevant opportunities + one<br>career insight|Habit through utility|



#### **9.1 Retention hierarchy** 

1. Personalized opportunity change 

2. Application or deadline state 

3. Career intelligence that reflects the user profile 

4. Learning-resource recommendations once the resource database exists 

5. Generic editorial content only as a supplement 

##### **Retention rule** 

If SINNARA has nothing genuinely useful for a user, silence is better than a notification.

### **16. Experimentation Architecture** 

Growth experiments should change one meaningful variable at a time and be evaluated on downstream value. Avoid optimizing click-through while harming activation or trust. 

|**Experiment**|**Primary hypothesis**|**Guardrail metric**|
|---|---|---|
|Opportunity-led landing page vs generic<br>homepage|Specific value converts better|Activation quality|
|CV-first signup vs form-first|Document-first reduces setup friction|Profile accuracy|
|Strong-match wording vs numeric score|Qualitative language improves trust|Apply click rate, report-error rate|
|Daily digest vs twice-weekly|Frequency affects useful return behavior|Opt-out rate|
|Deadline-first card hierarchy|Urgency improves action|Save/apply rate + trust|
|Partner-branded landing page|Context increases referred activation|Activation and retention|
|Roundup vs single-opportunity post|Different content densities optimize different<br>audiences|Qualified visits + activation|
|Career insight post vs pure job post|Educational content can broaden top-of-<br>funnel|Activation, not just engagement|



#### **16.1 Experiment rules** 

- Every experiment has a hypothesis, owner, start date, success metric and guardrail metric. 

- Do not run experiments on low-quality opportunity data. Bad inventory creates misleading growth results. 

- Do not expose experimental ranking policies to a small user subset if the candidate could be materially disadvantaged without review. 

- Store experiment key and variant in analytics events so every result is reproducible.
